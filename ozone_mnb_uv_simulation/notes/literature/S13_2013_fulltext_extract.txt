The reaction of NH 3 + O3 on the singlet potential energy surface has been 
investigated theoretically using CCSD(T) and G3B3 methods with the 
6‑311++G(3df,3pd) basis set based on the geometric parameters optimised at 
the B3LYP/6‑311++G(3df,3pd) level of theory. The calculated results revealed 
that the reactants are firstly associated with the adduct NH 3 – O3, through a 
barrier‑less process and two stable collision complexes, C1 and C2 have been 
considered. Six kinds of products P 1 (HOO + H2NO), P 2 (OH + H2NO2), P 3 
(O2 + H3NO), P4 (HNO + H2O2), P5 (HNO 2 + H2O) and P 6 (NO  + HO2 + H2) are 
obtained through a variety of transformations of C1 and C2. Thermodynamic 
parameters of all possible products show five production channels which are 
spontaneous. After taking into account the reaction barrier and enthalpy, 
the most probable reaction pathways are P1(2) and P4. Also, P 4 is the most 
stable product in comparison with the others and P 1 yields the other important 
products from the kinetic viewpoint; this is relatively stable, with a negative 
Gibbs free energy. Branching ratio results show P 1 is the major product at low 
temperature. Finally, the rates constant have been computed only for the P1(2) 
and P4 pathways by related theories in the temperature range of 200 – 2500 K.
KeywoRds: reaction mechanism, ammonia, rate constant, ozone

are powerful oxidising agents, far stronger than O2 [4], and therefore they have 
many industrial and consumer applications.

Ammonia is the most third abundant nitrogen species in the terrestrial 
atmosphere and plays a critical role in both homogeneous and heterogeneous 
atmospheric reactions. Being the dominant basic gas in the atmosphere, 
ammonia partially neutralises atmospheric acids in precipitation. Ammonia 
can be found in trace quantities in the atmosphere and is produced from the 
decay of nitrogenous animal and vegetable matter [5,6]; dozens of chemical 
plants worldwide also produce ammonia [7] and it has a major role as a non ‑
aqueous solvent [8]. Many physical and chemical mechanisms are responsible 
for the removal of NH3 from the atmosphere, chiefly these involve atmospheric 
moisture (e.g. clouds, rain, and fog) and surface water ( e.g. seas, rivers, lakes) 
and deposition on vegetation and soil.
NH3 catalyses the atmospheric oxidation of sulfur dioxide to sulfur trioxide 
and reacts rapidly with acidic components in the atmosphere ( e.g. sulfuric, 
nitric, and hydrochloric acids) [9]. The main atmosphere reaction of NH 3 is its 
heterogeneous reaction with acidic components such as aerosols with sulfuric 
acid, yielding NH 4HSO4 and (NH 4)2SO4, respectively. Also, its reaction with 
nitric acid forming NH 4NO3 particles is possible. It does react with ozone to 
form ammonium nitrate (NH 4NO3) as shown experimentally in [11] and the 
process is described by the following reactions [10,11]:
NH3 + 4O3 → H NO3 + 4O2 + H2O
NH3 + HNO3 → N H4NO3
In these reaction processes, NH 3 is first oxidised to nitric acid, and then reacts 
with nitric acid to form NH 4NO3. The kinetics of the reaction are a function 
of the initial stoichiometric ratio of NH 3 to O3 [10,11]. Olszyna et al. [11] have 
initiated a program to examine the kinetics of particle formation, i.e.  both the 
size distribution and growth rates, as a function of time for particles produced 
from gas ‑phase chemical reactions. In their work, NH 4NO3 particles were 
prepared from the reaction of NH 3 and O3 at room temperature. In all of their 
experiments, N2 was also present. One molecule of NH 4NO3 was presumed to 
be produced for every four molecules of O3 consumed.
Ammonia has been at the forefront of advances in refrigeration technology, 
making it essential to the food processing, storage and delivery infrastructure 
of our economy. The advantages of ammonia in refrigeration are well known. 
Ammonia does not destroy atmospheric ozone and does not contribute to the 
greenhouse effect linked to global warming. In fact, ammonia, one of the most 
common compounds found in nature, is essential to the earth’s nitrogen cycle 
and its release in the atmosphere is immediately recycled. The use of ammonia 
as a refrigerant is consistent with international agreements on reducing global

To the best of our knowledge, there is no direct experimental nor theoretical 
investigation that report the kinetic and thermodynamic data for ammonia 
and O3 reaction at room temperature. So, in this work, we have carried out 
a theoretical study on the mechanism of the NH 3 + O3 reaction with a 1:1

objective is to suggest the reaction mechanism and provide further information 
about the purely gaseous phase reaction of NH3 + O3 on the singlet PES.
2. coMPuTaTIonal deTaIls
All computations were carried out with the GAUSSIAN 03 suite of programs 
[12]. The geometrical parameters of the reactants (denoted as R), pre ‑reactive 
complexes (denoted by prefix C), transition states (denoted as TS), products, 
intermediates (denoted as IN), product complex (denoted as CP) in the NH3 + O3 
reaction have been optimised using density functional theory (DFT) with the 
Becke 3‑parameter hybrid exchange [13] and Lee–Yang–Parr [14] correlation 
density functional (B3LYP) in conjunction with the 6‑311++G (3df, 3pd) basis 
set.
Single‑point calculations were carried out at the CCSD (T) [15] level on the 
B3LYP optimised geometries. The CCSD (T) method is employed to compute 
the T1 diagnostic values for all the structures. In the CCSD, the wave function 
assesses the reliability of these calculations with regard to the possible 
multireference character of the wave function at the corresponding stationary 
point. In the case of the T1 diagnostic, the extent of multireference character in 
a given species is diagnosed by CCSD calculations, as described by Lee and 
Taylor [16]. A low T1 value indicates that the wave function is dominated by 
a single determinant. It has been suggested that the presence of large single 
amplitudes is problematic and, in general, a value in excess of 0.02 for the T 1 
diagnostic for the closed-shell species in question has significant multireference 
character. In the case of open‑shell species, it has been suggested that a higher 
threshold for the T 1 diagnostic may be more appropriate, and several recent 
studies [17 – 22] have shown that T1 diagnostic values for open‑shell systems of 
up to 0.045 may acceptable.

the computed stationary points are characterised according to the number of 
negative eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix at the B3LYP level. In this way, the

3.1 Initial association
Two stable collision complexes, C1 (O 3 – NH3) and C2 (O 3 – H3N), have been 
considered between NH3 and O3 on the singlet PES. The atoms terminal oxygen 
of ozone approach the N‑atom of NH3 to form C1 (O3 – NH3 which includes the 
four‑membered ring of OOON) (Figure 1). C2 (O3 – H3N) forms when the atoms 
terminal oxygen of ozone approach two H‑atoms of NH 3. This intermediate 
has a six‑membered ring of OOOHNH (Figure 1). The C1 and C2 species are 
1.41 and 0.57 kcal mol  – 1 below the original reactants at the CCSD (T) level. 
Therefore, C1 is only 0.84 kcal mol – 1 more stable than C2. No transition state 
has been found for C1 and C2 formation. So, their formation is a barrier ‑less 
process.

4. conclusIons
The theoretical study carried out in this work leads to the following conclusions:
1. In the present work, details of the reaction pathways of NH 3 + O3 on 
the singlet potential energy surfaces have been characterised with the 
B3LYP/6‑311++G (3df, 3pd), G3B3//B3LYP and CCSD//B3LYP levels.
2. Two stable collision complexes, C1 and C2 have been considered. C1 has 
a four‑membered‑ring structure, lying 1.41 kcal mol – 1 below the energy 
of the reactants at the CCSD (T) level. C2, lying 0.57 kcal mol  – 1 below 
the energy of reactants at the CCSD (T) level, has two hydrogen bonds.
3. Via a variety of transformations of C1 and C2, six kinds of products P 1 
(HO2 + H2NO), P2 (H 2NO2 + OH), P3 (O2 + H3NO), P4 (HNO + H2O2), P5 
(H2O + HNO2) and P6 (NO + HO2 + H2) are obtained.
4. P 4 is the most stable product in comparison with the others and P 1 is the 
other important products form the kinetic viewpoint, which is relatively 
stable with a negative Gibbs free energy.
5. Branching ratio results show P1 to be the major product when the kinetic 
variable is important (low temperature and initial time for the reaction).
